How to Write AI Video Prompts That Don't Look Fake

Most AI video prompts fail for the same reason: they try to do too much in one clip. A prompt like "a man builds a car, then drives it through the city at sunset" asks the model to juggle three scenes, two lighting setups, and a camera transition in eight seconds. The result is morphing faces, melting hands, and backgrounds that slide into each other. The fix is discipline: one subject, one action, one camera move, one setting per clip. If your video needs three actions, write three prompts. Your final video is a montage of short, clean clips — not one impossible mega-prompt.

The lock block: how to keep subjects consistent

AI video tools generate each clip independently, so "the same man" means nothing to them. You need a lock block — a dense, repeatable description of your subject that you paste verbatim at the top of every prompt: age, skin tone, hair, exact clothing, distinctive props. "Craftsman, mid-40s, weathered hands, rolled-up denim sleeves, amber safety glasses, leather tool belt." Change nothing between clips. Even a single changed adjective (denim → cotton) can shift the look. The generator above has a dedicated field for this because it is the single highest-impact habit in AI video.

Negatives: ban the failure modes

Every generator above includes a negatives block because certain defects are so common they deserve a standing ban: extra limbs, extra fingers, morphing faces, watermark text, on-screen captions, logo text. AI models love to hallucinate text on signs and labels — if your scene should have no readable text, say so explicitly. This one habit removes the defects that scream "AI-made" to viewers.

Common mistakes and quick fixes

Mistake 1: camera whiplash. Asking for "drone shot, then close-up, then orbit" in one clip guarantees a jarring mess. Fix: one camera move per clip; transitions happen in your edit. Mistake 2: dialogue in prompts. Lip-synced speech is still unreliable; keep clips visual-first and add voiceover in post. Mistake 3: vague subjects. "A nice car" gives you a random car; "honey-brown wooden 2-door vintage coupe, round headlights" gives you your car. Mistake 4: ignoring aspect ratio. A 16:9 cinematic prompt pasted into a 9:16 Shorts generator will crop the subject's face. Pick the platform first — the generator sets framing accordingly.

Putting it together: your workflow

One, write your lock block once and reuse it. Two, storyboard your video as 6–10 short clips, each with one action. Three, generate a prompt per clip with this tool. Four, generate clips and keep only the best takes. Five, edit in any free editor, add real voiceover or music, and review each clip for morphing before publishing. Creators who follow this pipeline consistently produce AI video that viewers cannot distinguish from filmed footage at Shorts scale — and every prompt starts in the box above.
