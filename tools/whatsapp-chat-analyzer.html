What This Analyzer Actually Tells You

Every WhatsApp chat has a hidden shape: who starts conversations, who replies in seconds and who replies tomorrow, whether you're both night owls or one of you does all the morning texting. This analyzer turns an exported chat into those numbers — message counts per person, share percentages, hourly and weekday rhythms, your most-used words and emoji, and median reply times.

It also finds the longest silence in the whole conversation, which is usually more funny than dramatic. These are descriptive statistics, not psychology: the numbers show what happened, and you decide what it means. Nothing here claims to read minds or judge relationships.

How to Export Your Chat on Android

Open the chat on your phone and tap the three dots in the top corner. Choose More, then Export chat, then Without media. Your phone saves a plain .txt file — that is the file you drop into the analyzer above. The whole thing takes about ten seconds.

Exports cap at roughly 40,000 messages, which covers years of even the chattiest friendships. If the chat is a group, every participant's messages come along in the same file, and the analyzer breaks the stats down per sender automatically.

How to Export Your Chat on iPhone

Open the chat, tap the contact or group name at the top of the screen, and scroll down to Export Chat. Choose Without Media and share the file to yourself — by email, AirDrop, or Files. iPhone wraps the export in a .zip archive, so open it and pick the _chat.txt inside.

That _chat.txt is the actual export; drop it into the analyzer. One thing to know: WhatsApp Web cannot export chats at all, so this has to be done on your phone. The steps above work on current iOS versions.

How to Use the Tool

Drop the .txt file into the box above, or click the box to browse for it. Everything happens instantly on your device: the parser reads the file, figures out each message's date, sender, and text, then builds the full dashboard — headline cards, charts, tables, and all.

If the dates look wrong — a chat from June showing up as May, for example — your export probably uses the other date order. Hit the date-order toggle to flip between DD/MM and MM/DD and the dashboard re-parses live. No account, no upload, no waiting room, no paywall at the end.

Privacy: Why "In Your Browser" Matters Here

A chat export is one of the most personal files you own — years of conversations with someone you care about, jokes, plans, arguments, all of it. Many analyzer tools ask you to upload that file to their server, which means your private messages end up sitting on a stranger's computer.

This page does the opposite. Your file is read with the browser's FileReader API and every calculation runs in JavaScript on your own device. Nothing is transmitted anywhere, nothing is stored, and there is no server on our end that ever sees a single message. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it still works.

Reading Your Results Honestly

Median reply time is the most interesting number on the dashboard. Unlike the average, it ignores the one time someone took three days and shows the typical case instead. "Conversation starters" counts who texts first after at least six quiet hours — a decent proxy for who keeps the chat alive.

The longest-silence stat is just for fun; a two-week gap usually means somebody went on holiday, not that the friendship ended. And keep an eye on the unparsed-lines counter: if your export uses an unusual timestamp format, a few lines may be skipped, and the dashboard tells you exactly how many so nothing is hidden.

Group Chats Are Where It Shines

One-on-one chats are fun, but group chats are where the per-sender breakdown earns its keep: who carries the group, who only appears for memes, whose reply time suggests they muted the chat months ago. Share percentages make the group dynamics visible at a glance.

The copy-summary button produces a paste-ready text summary of the whole analysis — the kind of thing people screenshot and drop back into the group itself.
