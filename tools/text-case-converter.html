Why Reformat Text Instead of Retyping It

Pasted text rarely arrives clean. An email arrives in ALL CAPS because someone left Caps Lock on. A list copied from a spreadsheet carries the same name five times. A headline drafted in lowercase needs title case before it can go on a page. Retyping fixes all of it, but retyping is slow and introduces new typos — the exact problem you were trying to solve.

A case converter and duplicate remover fixes the formatting without touching the meaning. You paste once, pick the style, and copy the result back. The words stay yours; only the casing and the repeats change. For students cleaning up essay drafts, creators tidying captions and tag lists, and office workers de-duplicating mailing lists, that one paste saves the most boring ten minutes of the day.

Title Case has one subtlety worth knowing: small words like "and", "of", and "the" stay lowercase in the middle of a title but are capitalized at the start or end. This tool follows that rule, so "the lord of the rings" becomes "The Lord of the Rings". Acronyms are a different story — "NASA" becomes "Nasa" under Title Case, so fix short acronyms by hand after converting.

How Duplicate Line Removal Works

Duplicate removal works line by line. The tool reads your text one line at a time and keeps the first occurrence of each line, in the original order. Later repeats are dropped, and a counter tells you exactly how many lines were removed — so you can check the result against what you expected. Turning on "ignore case" treats "Apple" and "apple" as the same line, which matters when a list was typed by different people.

Two options change the result. "Trim spaces on each line" strips invisible spaces from the start and end of every line, so " apple" and "apple" count as duplicates. "Remove empty lines" drops blank lines entirely. If you need a sorted list instead of the original order, tick "Sort A–Z" — the tool dedupes first, then sorts, so the output is clean and alphabetical. Check the counts before copying; for a mailing list, count your output against your expected contacts.

Mistakes That Break Your Text

The biggest mistake is converting twice. Running Title Case over text that is already in Title Case is harmless, but running it after Sentence case — or the other way around — can mangle proper nouns and acronyms. Convert once, then fix names and acronyms by hand. The second mistake is deduping with "ignore case" off when the list mixes "Apple" and "apple": you will keep both and think the list is clean.

The third mistake is trusting the tool with text it should not change. Code, passwords, and case-sensitive IDs must never go through a case converter — "PassWord123" and "password123" are different passwords. This page runs entirely in your browser, so nothing you paste is uploaded, but case changes are permanent once you copy over the original. Keep the original somewhere until you have checked the result.
