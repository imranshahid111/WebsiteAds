The money you make: pricing and profitability

1. Profit Margin Calculator — is it worth selling?

Every product or service you sell has two numbers that matter: gross margin (revenue minus cost of goods) and net margin (what survives after overhead). The gross tells you if the price is right; the net tells you if the business survives. If you only track one number, make it net margin.

Run every product through it at least once a quarter — a $49.99 product with $22 in costs and $3,000 in monthly overhead needs volume you can verify. Our free profit margin calculator gives you both margins instantly. Anything under ~10% net needs a price rise, a cost cut, or a retirement.

2. Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator — what should I charge?

Service businesses routinely undercharge because they divide a salary goal by 2,080 working hours — ignoring unpaid time off, unbillable hours, and self-employment tax. The result looks reasonable and quietly loses money. The correct floor is (income goal + expenses + tax reserve) divided by actual billable hours.

Use it before every proposal, and rerun it whenever your goals or costs change — our guide walks through the full freelance pricing formula with a worked $109/hr example. Quote 10-20% above the floor, because scope always creeps and proposals get negotiated down.

The money you manage: budgets, borrowing, savings

3. 50/30/20 Budget Calculator — where does the owner's pay go?

Owners often treat business and personal money as one pot, which hides both problems. A clean personal budget — 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings — tells you exactly what salary the business must pay you to sustain your life. That number becomes your real break-even.

Run it monthly on your actual take-home and adjust the split for high-cost cities or variable income — our 50/30/20 budget calculator does the split in seconds, and the guide shows what to do when the rule does not fit your reality.

4. Loan / EMI Calculator — what does borrowing really cost?

Equipment loans, working-capital lines, expansion borrowing — every one looks cheaper in the headline rate than it is in the monthly payment. An EMI calculator converts the rate and term into the number that actually hits your account, so you can check it against your margin first.

Before signing anything, enter the amount, rate, and term, then compare the total interest across terms — a shorter term costs more monthly but saves thousands overall. Our free loan EMI calculator shows the payment, total interest, and the balance you will actually carry.

5. Savings Goal Calculator — when is the safety net ready?

Every small business needs a cash cushion — the standard advice is 3 to 6 months of expenses — plus funded expansion goals. A savings calculator turns a vague target into a monthly deposit and a date, with interest included so the timeline is realistic rather than aspirational.

Set the goal, your monthly deposit, and your interest rate, then check the timeline monthly — if it says 14 months, you can decide today whether to raise the deposit or shrink the goal. Our free savings goal calculator shows exactly when each milestone lands.

6. PDF Bank Statement to Excel — stop retyping statements

Bookkeeping is the chore owners postpone until tax season, and the biggest friction is getting bank statements into a spreadsheet. Converting a PDF statement by hand is an evening of retyping and typos; doing it automatically is a two-minute job.

Upload the PDF, preview the extracted rows, delete any stray lines, and download the CSV — our free PDF bank statement to Excel tool runs entirely in your browser, so nothing uploads. Do this monthly and reconciliation becomes a 20-minute habit instead of a January crisis.

The time you save: marketing, security, and admin

Marketing copy lives under length limits: ad headlines, meta descriptions, email subject lines, social posts. Paste the draft into a word counter and you know instantly whether it fits the channel — plus reading time, which matters for landing pages.

8. Strong Password Generator — protect the business

Your business accounts — banking, payroll, email, the store admin — are worth far more than a personal login. One reused password across them is the cheapest way to get robbed. Every account needs its own long, random password, and nobody should memorize them.

Generate a fresh 16+ character password for each business account and store them in a password manager — our free strong password generator creates them instantly. This is a 15-minute job that eliminates the single most common small-business security failure.

How to actually use all ten

Do not try to adopt all ten at once. Start with the profit margin calculator today — it tells you which products deserve your energy. Add the pricing and budget calculators this week, the savings and loan ones before your next borrowing decision, and fold the time savers into existing habits as they come up.

The whole set takes under an hour to learn and minutes to run each time. The payoff is not the calculators themselves — it is that every business decision starts from a number instead of a guess. Guesses are free, but they are the most expensive kind of free.

Frequently asked questions

Which free calculators should every small business owner use?

At minimum: a profit margin calculator (per-product profitability), a pricing or freelance rate calculator (what to charge), a budget calculator (where owner pay goes), and a loan/EMI calculator (before borrowing). Add a savings goal calculator for an emergency fund and a bank-statement-to-spreadsheet tool for bookkeeping, and you cover the core money decisions.

Are free online calculators accurate enough for business decisions?

Yes for planning and comparison — the math behind margin, EMI, and budget splits is standardized. Treat a calculator as a fast, correct answer to a well-defined question, not as a replacement for an accountant on tax, legal, or audited financials. Run the numbers yourself, then bring them to your accountant as a starting point.

What is the most important calculator for a new business?

The profit margin calculator. Everything else — pricing, budgeting, borrowing — is guesswork until you know whether each product or service actually makes money after all costs. Run every product through it at least once a quarter, and rerun it whenever costs or prices change.

Can a calculator replace an accountant for a small business?

No. Calculators handle the arithmetic — margins, payments, savings timelines — fast and free. An accountant handles judgment: tax strategy, entity choice, deductions, compliance, and spotting problems in your books. The best setup is both: use calculators to understand your numbers, and use an accountant to make them official and optimized.

How often should a small business owner run these calculations?

Profit margin and pricing: quarterly, or whenever costs or prices change. Budget and savings progress: monthly. Loan/EMI: before signing any borrowing, and again if rates move. The whole set takes under an hour, and it turns vague financial anxiety into specific numbers you can act on.
