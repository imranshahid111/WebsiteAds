What a Savings Goal Calculator Does

Every savings goal has three honest answers: how long it takes, how much you will put in, and how much the bank pays you along the way. This calculator answers all three in seconds. Type in a goal — an emergency fund, a car down payment, a vacation, a new laptop — plus what you have saved today, what you can deposit each month, and your account's interest rate, and it works out the exact month your balance crosses the finish line.

The inputs, explained

Goal: the total number you need. Be specific and include the extras people forget — a $12,000 car goal usually needs another $1,500 for tax, title, and insurance. Current savings: what is sitting in the account today, before this plan starts. Monthly deposit: what you can realistically add every single month, not your best month. Interest rate: enter your account's APY — the effective yearly rate after compounding. This is the number on your bank's website, not the rate on your loan paperwork (that's APR, a different thing).

The math: how compound interest works

The calculator compounds your interest monthly, the way real savings accounts do. Each month your balance grows a little from the interest, and next month you earn interest on that slightly bigger balance — the classic snowball. The formula is: balance = current × (1 + r)n + monthly × ((1 + r)n − 1) / r, where r is the monthly rate and n the number of months. If your rate is 0%, it simply adds up the deposits. The result is the shortest whole month in which your balance reaches the goal — so a result of 41 months means month 40 still falls short.

A worked example

Say you want a $10,000 emergency fund, you have $1,000 saved, you can deposit $200 a month, and your high-yield account pays 5% APY. The calculator shows 41 months (3 years 5 months): you will deposit $9,200 total and earn about $908 in interest. Without interest it would take 45 months — compounding hands you back nearly four months for free. That free money is why where you park your savings matters as much as how much you deposit.

How to hit your goal faster

1. Automate the deposit on payday. A transfer that happens before you see the money never gets skipped; it is the single most effective habit in personal finance. 2. Move the money to a high-yield account. As of 2026, traditional big-bank savings accounts still pay close to 0%, while online high-yield accounts pay roughly 4–5% APY. On a $10,000 multi-year goal that difference is worth hundreds of dollars — and zero extra effort. 3. Raise the deposit by $50. In the example above, $200 becomes $250 a month and the goal arrives about 7 months sooner. 4. Feed it your windfalls. Tax refunds, bonuses, and sold gadgets go straight in — a single $1,000 windfall can erase five months of waiting. 5. Find the money in your budget. Run our 50/30/20 budget calculator first: most people find $100–200 a month hiding in subscriptions and food delivery that can be redirected straight into the savings goal.

When saving should wait: kill the expensive debt first

If you carry credit-card debt at 20%+ interest, saving at 4.5% while paying 20% is losing money in slow motion. Keep a small $1,000 starter cushion, then attack the high-rate debt before building a big goal fund. Our loan EMI calculator shows exactly how much that debt costs you per month — compare that number against what your savings earn and the right order becomes obvious.

Next steps after your calculation

Run your numbers above, then lock them in: set the automatic transfer for the day after payday and move the goal money into a high-yield account today. Recheck every 3 months — if you got a raise, bump the deposit and watch the timeline shrink. If the timeline is longer than you can accept, split the goal: a 70%-funded emergency fund plus a started vacation fund beats one perfect, never-started plan.
