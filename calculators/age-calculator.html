How Old Are You, Exactly?

Most people answer "I'm 36" when asked their age — but that number is up to 364 days wrong. Your exact age is three numbers: years, months, and days. A person born May 15, 1990 is 36 years, 5 months, and 5 days old on October 20, 2026. That precision matters more often than you'd think: insurance quotes, retirement eligibility, school enrollment cutoffs, and legal age gates all run on exact dates, not rounded years.

Why you can't just subtract the years

Take 2026 − 1990 = 36 years — so far so good. But what about the months and days? October 20 minus May 15 is 5 months and 5 days — easy. Now try birthdays like January 31 counted on March 1: February only has 28 or 29 days, so the "months" part has to borrow from the year, exactly like subtraction in school. Our calculator borrows the real length of each month, so the answer is exact rather than a rough "years times 365" estimate.

Leap years and February 29

Leap-day babies get a real birthday only every four years, so calculators have to choose a convention. We use the one most US states follow for legal age: the birthday is observed on February 28 in non-leap years. On that day the calculator shows your new age, and the countdown targets the next February 28 (or 29 in leap years). A few jurisdictions use March 1 instead — the difference is a single day, once every four years.

Age in different units

Years, months, days is the human answer — but total days is the computer answer, and it's useful too. 13,307 days is easier to compare than "36 years, 5 months, 5 days" when you want stats like average sleep hours, or when a doctor asks how old a baby is (pediatricians count in weeks and months, never years). Total hours and minutes are just those days multiplied out — impressive numbers, but the same fact dressed up.

Time zones and the "as of" date

Leave the "as of" field blank and the calculator uses today. Entering a future date answers questions like "how old will I be when my loan is paid off?" — and yes, you can pair that with our loan EMI calculator to see the payoff month, then see your age on that exact day. One technical note: the calculator reads dates without time zones, so what you type is what it counts — no off-by-one-day surprises from midnight UTC conversions.

Three things people actually use exact age for

1. Retirement math. "I'll retire at 65" is a date, not a vibe. Plug your birthdate, set the "as of" field to your target retirement date, and see exactly how long you have — then run our savings goal calculator to find the monthly deposit that gets you there. 2. Life-stage budgets. The 50/30/20 rule splits differently at 25 (heavy savings) and 55 (heavy needs) — your age tells you which season you're in.

3. Official cutoffs. School enrollment ("must be 5 by September 1"), driving age, voting age, senior discounts — every one of these is a date comparison, and a one-day mistake means a rejected form. When a date cutoff matters, calculate on the exact cutoff date using the "as of" field, and print the result for the file.
